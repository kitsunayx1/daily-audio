## 1. Top three things to know

1. **Binance lost about $6.3 billion in the largest crypto theft on record; TRM attributes it to North Korea's TraderTraitor.**
   - From 20:05 UTC on 29 Oct, Binance hot wallets on ten blockchains were drained within about 20 minutes, with a second wave at about 23:05 UTC. TRM puts the total at **$6.36B**; Chainalysis estimates $5.912B; Binance says about $6.3B.
   - About **$2.7–3B was frozen** (Tether 971.1M USDT, Circle 823M USDC, Ripple, World Liberty Financial, and Arbitrum's Security Council). About **$2.4B** in ETH and BTC cannot be frozen.
   - TRM: TraderTraitor, **high confidence**. No FBI or US government attribution yet. Binance first said a **compromised DevOps engineer's laptop** may have given access to a service container, then found "additional points of breach". It says user funds are safe and it will cover losses.
   - **Action:** finance and treasury teams with Binance exposure should review it; expect Binance-themed phishing; for any firm with signing or payment systems, check who can reach them from a developer laptop. Deep dive in section 12.
2. **Law firms again, now in Canada, and Ottawa has issued a warning about the method.**
   - **McCarthy Tétrault** detected a "coordinated social engineering campaign" on 22 Oct; files including client information were accessed (an unnamed source told The Globe and Mail 31,000 files). **Davies Ward Phillips & Vineberg** reports one account compromised on 23 Oct. Method reported: phishing and fake IT staff on Microsoft Teams. No actor named.
   - The **Canadian Centre for Cyber Security (AL26-028)** warns of help-desk voice phishing against legal and financial firms: fake "passkey migration" calls, branded relay pages, attacker MFA devices, then Microsoft 365 data theft via Graph and extortion.
   - **Silent Ransom Group** listed **Susman Godfrey** (US trial firm) and a new redacted "L…". **Troutman Pepper Locke** filed formal notices (about 37,000 people per a class action). A forum post claims **260 GB of PwC Azure data and GitHub repositories** for sale (snippet only; separate from Everest's claim).
   - **Accenture:** WIRED reports (via secondary sources) that a contractor gave a personal Claude Code agent admin credentials on a Defense Department NIPRNet system for about two months. Accenture says the allegations were investigated and "found to be without merit".
   - **Action:** compare our help-desk identity checks and MFA-reset process with the Canadian alert; check McCarthy Tétrault, Davies, Susman Godfrey and Troutman Pepper Locke against the vendor and client database.
3. **CISA adds GitLab and Cisco firewalls to KEV with a Sunday deadline; physical systems are now in the frame.**
   - **GitLab CVE-2026-97229** (CVSS 10; forensic triage required) and **Cisco ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418**, added 29 Oct, both **due 1 Nov**.
   - **PAN-OS:** all PHP2Shell hotfixes but one (11.1.7-h12, due today) are out; Unit 42 says about 0.02% of the installed base had internet-facing management interfaces and was impacted.
   - **US military commissaries:** freezers at six or seven bases switched to defrost in August; on 29 Oct Reuters reported the Pentagon is treating it as a suspected cyberattack, with links being explored to the mid-October French supermarket refrigeration attacks. **Belgium's tax agency** pulled its portals after a cyberattack.
   - **Action:** patch GitLab and the Cisco firewalls before the weekend; ask facilities which building and refrigeration controllers are reachable from the internet.

**[RESOLVED] Arista VeloCloud CVE numbers:** the zero-day Lumen lists for Silent Train (CVE-2026-16812) is a different flaw from the one covered on 23 Oct (CVE-2026-114625). Both are exploited; patch both. See section 4.

## 5. Software supply chain

### GitHub issues used to inject commands into CI (Radware)
- 83 malicious issues, PRs and comments from 74 accounts (many likely compromised) hit 61 public repositories, 77 of them within 43 minutes on 1 Sep. Commands hidden in HTML comments ran through issue-triggered workflows that pasted text into shell scripts, sending environment variables out. Execution confirmed in two cases. Named repos include aquasecurity/trivy, google/langextract, mlflow, cilium, brave-browser, dotnet/maui, Homebrew/homebrew-cask and github/docs.
- **Action:** review any of our GitHub workflows triggered by issues or comments that interpolate user text into shell.

## 12. Threat actor deep dive: TraderTraitor and the Binance theft (29–30 Oct)

### Summary
- From 20:05 UTC on 29 Oct, Binance hot wallets on ten chains sent out about **$6.36B** (TRM, valued at transfer time; Binance about $6.3B; Chainalysis $5.912B). About $2.7B was frozen by 00:30 UTC on 30 Oct (TRM; Binance says about $3B). About $2.4B cannot be frozen (420,000+ ETH and 18,270 BTC). Deposits are back; withdrawals were expected to resume within about 45 hours of Binance's latest update.
- **Why it matters beyond crypto:** the likely route, from an engineer's laptop to control of a signing system, is the same route into any enterprise's payment, treasury or code-signing infrastructure.

### Attribution and confidence
- TRM: **TraderTraitor (DPRK), high confidence**, citing the playbook seen at Bybit (Feb 2025, $1.5B, FBI-attributed) and KelpDAO (Apr 2026): small Tornado Cash withdrawals to fund an operator wallet, staging well ahead, pre-signed transactions fired in a burst. Chainalysis also ties it to the group. Binance has named no attacker; no FBI statement yet.

### Timeline (UTC, per TRM)
- **28 Oct morning:** three small Tornado Cash withdrawals fund the operator wallet.
- **29 Oct early:** attack code tested on two wallets, rehearsed on a third; about four hours later installed on a Binance hot wallet.
- **20:05–20:09:** 233.46M XRP sent from five XRPL wallets. **20:10:** Solana and Optimism drained. **20:20:** 18,270 BTC leaves; one transaction submits **50 pre-signed EIP-7702 authorisations** on the Ethereum hot wallet. **About 20:23:** ETH hot wallet swept (167,464 ETH plus 63 tokens including 1.615B USDT); more sweeps to 23:56.
- **21:01:** Tether freezes 971.1M USDT. **21:04:** Circle freezes 823M USDC. **22:01:** Ripple freezes 40.5M RLUSD. **22:10:** World Liberty Financial freezes 27.8M USD1. **23:05:** second wave on Avalanche and Polygon.
- **30 Oct 00:08:** Arbitrum's Security Council moves 8,426 ETH and 74.6M ARB out of the attacker's wallet.

### Initial access (unconfirmed)
- Binance first said a **compromised DevOps engineer's laptop** may have given access to a service container used to submit withdrawals; later it found "additional points of breach" and said the incident "stems from multiple factors"; it found no evidence of a private-key leak. Co-CEO Richard Teng: not a single "one-click" action. Root-cause report pending.
- TRM's assessment: the attacker most likely gained the ability to make Binance's **hot-wallet signing system** sign transactions of its choosing; signatures across different chains, key types and schemes were produced within about 30 minutes, which phishing does not explain.

### Tooling and execution
- An attacker-only sweeper contract; EIP-7702 delegation authorisations tied to consecutive nonces so one would always be valid, turning the hot wallet into a sweeper that keeps forwarding new deposits; ordinary signed transfers on the other chains.

### Laundering
- About 325M USDT sold for about 122,300 ETH via UniswapX and intent-based solvers at a 10%+ discount; 60M USDT deposited on Aave to borrow about 19,760 ETH (Aave then froze the markets); 300M USDT to JustLend on Tron; BTC through a peel chain. TRM expects bridges, swap services and OTC brokers next.

### IOCs
- TRM has labelled the attacker addresses but **published no addresses, transaction hashes or host IOCs**; Binance has published none either. Watch TRM, Chainalysis and FBI releases.

### Hunting (enterprise-relevant)
1. Developer and DevOps endpoints with access to production containers: new remote-access tools, unusual Node or Python packages, interactive shells into signing or payment service containers outside change windows.
2. Signing services: bursts of signatures across several key types, or requests that skip the normal approval path.
3. Treasury wallets: any EIP-7702 delegation, or requests for many authorisations across a range of nonces.
4. Inbound payments and counterparties: screen against labelled TraderTraitor addresses through a blockchain-analytics provider.

### Response
- Rotate credentials for engineers who can reach signing or payment paths; re-image suspect laptops; review container exec and audit logs from 26 Oct.
- Separate signing infrastructure and approval paths by system and value tier (TRM's recommendation).
- Firms with Binance exposure: review counterparty and client-asset exposure; warn staff about Binance-themed phishing.

### MITRE ATT&CK (our researcher's mapping; neither TRM nor Binance gives one)
T1078 Valid Accounts; T1566 / T1195 (suspected route onto the laptop, typical of TraderTraitor, unconfirmed); T1609 Container Administration Command; T1583 Acquire Infrastructure (operator wallets and contracts); T1657 Financial Theft.
