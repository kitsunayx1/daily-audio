## 12. Deep dive: CL-STA-1238 and the "Cairn" autonomous intrusion framework (Unit 42, 12 Nov 2026) [NEW]

### Sources
- https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/cairn-framework-ai-autonomous-attacks/ (published about 15:00 UTC 12 Nov; no named author)
- https://helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/12/cairn-autonomous-ai-cyberattacks

### Why it matters
- One operator, or a small team, ran 25 operations against at least 19 organisations within about two weeks and got in at 10; the AI supplied the scale.
- Targets overlap with consulting's client base: semiconductor and advanced manufacturing, defence and defence policy, standards bodies.
- Every way in was mundane: weak or shared passwords, default credentials, open registration, IDOR, mass assignment, exposed cloud storage and APIs, old CVEs. These are typical of client-facing portals, microsites and partner apps built fast and left running.
- Third agentic framework in real intrusions in two weeks (after ARTEX and AntiFox); the first Unit 42 ties to a state-nexus collection mission. Unit 42: AI raised volume and coverage, not sophistication.

### Attribution and confidence
- CL-STA-1238, "China-nexus intelligence collection", **moderate confidence**.
- Basis: Chinese-language tasking, objective and authorisation files and operator UI; exclusive use of Chinese models (Zhipu GLM 5.2, DeepSeek V4 Flash and V4 Pro); victim selection aligned with Chinese state priorities.
- Not linked to a named APT; no source links it to VAPOR PANDA (ARTEX) or other clusters.

### Victims (sectors only)
- 7 semiconductor and advanced-manufacturing organisations (lithography, chip design, materials, photomask, standards).
- 3 defence and defence policy (a space-technology company, a defence-analytics vendor, a policy think tank).
- 4 human rights and civil society (a Canadian human-rights body, a US teachers' union, a science-policy group, an education nonprofit).
- [CONFLICT, within the source] Unit 42 says 10 confirmed compromised, but its case descriptions add up to 9.

### Timeline
- Aug–Sep 2026; simultaneous attempts against 19 organisations over about two weeks; individual operations ran from about 20 minutes to more than 50 hours. Published 12 Nov.

### Initial access (as published)
- Password spraying an internet-facing app server (standards body); unauthenticated cloud-storage write plus login-as-any-customer flaws (standards body).
- Brute-forcing a production credential valid across several accounts on a partner-integration app (semiconductor company).
- Unauthenticated account-creation bypass on a CMS (teachers' union); open backend registration endpoint on an identity platform (think tank).
- Pre-loaded VPN gateway addresses, group names and **shared group passwords** (equipment supplier), suggesting earlier reconnaissance or purchase.
- No specific CVE IDs given.

### Attack chain
- Operator creates a task with the target's root domain and a one-line Chinese goal (webshells, SSH, source code, keys, credentials, internal network entry).
- Customised fork of public Cairn (github.com/oritera/Cairn): bootstrap; **Reason** (model reviews a shared "blackboard" of facts and intents); **Explore** (one action, result written back as a fact); repeat until goal met or stopped; operator can inject hints.
- Phases (3–7 per target): recon and asset mapping; web-app exploitation; services and middleware; credential and secret collection; privilege escalation and lateral movement; internal access and persistence.
- Guardrail bypass: a Chinese preamble on every call asserting authorised pentest, CTF or audit work and forbidding refusal, plus a per-operation **AUTHORIZATION.md**. Neither model refused; Unit 42 calls the pretext "trivial".
- Models: GLM 5.2 in 13 operations, DeepSeek V4 Flash in 8, V4 Pro in 4 (apparently for targets that resisted Flash).

### Outcomes (as published)
- Standards body: dozens of valid credentials, non-public directories, **a live standards-ballot document overwritten**, access to meeting, voting and back-office functions.
- Semiconductor company: app data, user and email lists, **new admin accounts and a persistent production API token**.
- US teachers' union: over 1M member records, about 31,000 CRM contacts, staff password hashes.
- Defence-policy think tank / analytics vendor: about 500 user records incl. .mil and .gov accounts.
- CMS cases: employee data at a lithography manufacturer; about 150,000 internal documents at an industrial-automation manufacturer; student data at an education nonprofit.

### Tooling
- Cairn fork with operator web UI (Chinese login page); custom "AIScan Runner Skills" pack not in upstream. No scanners, commands, user-agents, ports or webshell names published.

### Infrastructure (defanged, as published)
- 154.44.31[.]4: Cairn hosting server (HK, AS979, NetLab Global)
- 82.29.129[.]54: staging and attack egress (US, AS142036, Hosteons Pte. Ltd.)
- No domains, URLs or file hashes published.

### Hunting (our derivation from Unit 42's narrative; Unit 42 gave no queries)
1. Search firewall, proxy, WAF, CDN and IdP logs for the two IPs, Aug 2026 to now.
2. Machine-speed probing of internet-facing apps: many distinct paths and parameters per minute from one source for hours or days, mixing recon, auth attempts and object-ID walking.
3. Password spraying against app logins (partner portals, CMS admin, VPN group logins), not only the IdP.
4. New accounts via self-registration on CMSs and identity platforms that should be closed, especially if then elevated.
5. New admin users and long-lived API tokens in production apps; unauthenticated writes to cloud storage.
6. Bulk export of member, CRM or user tables after a new session.

### Response
- Unit 42: identity hygiene (no default or shared credentials), attack-surface reduction (internet-facing apps, registration endpoints, cloud storage), patch old CVEs, fix auth bypass, IDOR and mass assignment, JIT access and zero standing privilege.
- Ours: close self-registration on client-facing portals and CMSs; rotate shared VPN group passwords; review admin accounts and API tokens created since Aug 2026 on externally reachable apps, including engagement microsites.
