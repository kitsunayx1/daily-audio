## 1. Top three things to know

1. **Three edge products are under active attack, and CISA deadlines fall this week.**
   - **Palo Alto PAN-OS, CVE-2026-0322:** root code execution through the management interface. The vendor reports "very limited exploitation". It is in CISA's KEV catalogue with a 17 Oct deadline.
   - **Cisco Secure Email Gateway, CVE-2026-76519 (CVSS 9.8):** one crafted email attachment can lead to root on the gateway. It is exploited, and AsyncOS 16.0 has no fix.
   - **Ivanti Connect Secure:** honeypots now see mass scanning. CISA's deadline is 16 Oct and its guidance is a factory reset if you suspect compromise.
   - **Action:** check exposure to all three today. Keep management interfaces off the internet.
2. **Microsoft and partners took down three Chinese-language phishing-kit platforms.**
   - The platforms were CoGUI, Haozi and Iron Man System, plus the YAM Store tenant marketplace.
   - They sent more than 1 billion messages and impersonated more than 75,000 organisations through about 17,000 domains.
   - Brand-impersonation phishing will dip, then come back under new names. YAM Store also sold compromised Microsoft and Google tenants.
3. **Ransomware is getting personal.**
   - A Semperis survey says 37% of ransomware attacks involved threats against staff: doxxing, swatting, and threats to family and children.
   - Separately, leaked Revolut KYC data may be linked to an armed home invasion in the UK. That link is unconfirmed.
   - **Action:** a conversation with executive protection about partners' home addresses and family exposure online.

## 4. Exploited and critical vulnerabilities

### Palo Alto PAN-OS management interface: CVE-2026-0322 (exploited, KEV)
- Authenticated code injection leading to root on PA-Series and VM-Series firewalls and Panorama. Cloud NGFW and Prisma Access are not affected.
- **Score:** CVSS 4.0 of 8.6, with the vendor's highest urgency.
- **Fixed in:** 12.2.4, 12.1.10-h2, 11.1.16-h3, 10.2.18-h12, plus 11.2 hotfixes. There is no workaround; restrict the management interface to trusted IPs.
- **IOC IPs:** 66.42.72[.]14, 208.83.239[.]114, 149.248.47[.]234.
- **KEV:** added 15 Oct, federal deadline 17 Oct.
- **Not the same flaw as** the GlobalProtect CVE-2026-0257 covered on 13 Oct.
- **Urgency:** patch within 24 to 48 hours.
- https://www.securityweek.com/palo-alto-networks-patches-pan-os-zero-day-exploited-for-remote-code-execution/
- https://cybersecuritynews.com/pan-os-0-day-vulnerability/

### Cisco Secure Email Gateway: CVE-2026-76519 (CVSS 9.8, exploited)
- An unauthenticated attacker sends a crafted attachment, writes files to the system and gets root.
- **Affected when:** File Reputation is enabled and Content Scanner Tools is older than 25.3.0.5525.
- **Fix:** AsyncOS 15.5.5-014 or 16.5.0-780, or Content Scanner Tools 25.3.0.5525+ (no reboot needed). 16.0 has no fix, so migrate. Rebuild any compromised virtual appliances.
- **Related:** Cisco also says the September SQL-injection flaw CVE-2026-76461 is exploited. The China-linked UAT-9686 hit this product line earlier.
- **Urgency:** critical. Check Content Scanner versions and look for "System Security" log messages.
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisco-warns-of-active-attacks.html
- https://www.securityweek.com/cisco-warns-of-exploited-secure-email-gateway-vulnerability/
- https://cybersecuritynews.com/cisco-secure-email-gateway-0-day-vulnerability/

### [UPDATE] Ivanti Connect Secure: CVE-2026-104935
- **Mass scanning:** Previdian logged 105 exploit attempts from 50 IPs in Singapore and the US. It expects fully weaponised exploitation within days. No public PoC has been confirmed.
- **CISA guidance if compromise is suspected:** take a configuration backup, factory reset, upgrade, restore, then reset all credentials, certificates and API keys. Deadline 16 Oct.
- **End of life:** 22.8 reaches end of life on 31 Jan 2027.
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/cisa-urges-immediate-ivanti-connect.html
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisa-warns-admins-to-immediately-fix-ivanti-flaw-exploited-as-zero-day/

### Nextcloud "Built-in CODE" app (Collabora): CVE-2026-110447 (exploited since 10 Sep)
- Unauthenticated command injection in `proxy.php` of `richdocumentscode` before 26.4.303. It can only be triggered while the local document server is down.
- Attackers dropped PHP webshells (e.g. `shxf4645.php`), hunted for password-manager files and crypto wallets, and sent data out to webhook[.]site.
- **IOCs:** 139.28.18[.]122 and 89.38.98[.]50. Webshell SHA-256: `4b9fbd0663fbfd6028fc216ed18e0d6ef3ae498a7fdc97083e4b0ce3cec40562`.
- **Fix:** update or remove the app. Disabling it is **not** enough.
- **Why it matters:** clients and third parties self-host Nextcloud for document exchange.
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/hackers-backdoor-nextcloud-servers.html

## 11. Threat actor deep dive: PEERSTITCH, a memory-only FortiGate implant (Unit 42; CL-STA-0954 / UNC5820)

- **Who:** an unnamed cluster. Its activity overlaps with suspected nation-state intrusions, and it shows deep knowledge of FortiOS internals.
- **Victim:** a telecom managed service provider, compromised in September 2024. One FortiManager exposed configurations and credentials for more than 500 managed FortiGates, including ones protecting the provider's clients.
- **Initial access:**
  - CVE-2024-47575 "FortiJump" (FortiManager FGFM, TCP 541): the actor registered a rogue FortiGate and stole the configuration archive.
  - CVE-2025-61624: FortiOS auto-script path traversal, used to write files as root.
  - Possibly CVE-2024-26009 and CVE-2024-26008 (medium confidence).
- **Implant:**
  - An ARM64 injector (`lldprx`) patches the `miglogd` and `iked` daemons in memory using ptrace.
  - The two daemons talk to each other through `/proc/<pid>/mem` writes.
  - Tagged VPN sessions (markers 8550 and 169.254.0.1) skip FortiOS security checks, and integrity checks are disabled.
  - No binary on disk, no listening port, and no outbound connections of its own.
- **Persistence and C2:**
  - A rogue Apache mod_md ACME config polls a fake ACME server at 23.240.77[.]233:8084.
  - Commands arrive hidden in the DNS-01 challenge field, run via `/bin/node`, and open an SSH reverse shell.
  - The actor impersonated FortiGateCloud to pull configurations.
  - It captured RADIUS, LDAP and TFTP traffic, and brute-forced reversible secrets to recover admin and SSL-VPN passwords.
- **Anti-forensics:** killed logging daemons, wiped crash logs, and deleted rogue admin accounts and VPN groups.
- **IOCs:**
  - IPs: 23.240.77[.]233, 47.18.167[.]159, 45.32.41[.]202, 198.199.122[.]22.
  - Staging URL: hxxp://23.240.77[.]233:8085/pM.js.
  - Files: `pM.js`, `index.js`, `index.wasm`, `ipssem.js`, `/data2/template/`.
  - YARA rule: `u42_apt_linux_PEERSTITCH_fortigate_implant`. Hashes are in the report.
- **Hunting:**
  - Anonymous 16,384-byte `rwxp` memory regions in `miglogd` or `iked`.
  - Signal 11 crash logs for those daemons.
  - `../` in auto-scripts.
- **Response:** patch both CVEs, then rotate every credential and certificate held by FortiManager. Ask managed service providers and managed-firewall vendors whether they run FortiManager and have hunted for this.
- **Source:** https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/fortimanager-cve-2024-47575-peerstitch-malware/
