## 1. Top three things to know

1. **[CORRECTION] The European government intrusion we led with on Monday is no longer linked to PHP2Shell. But PHP2Shell is now in KEV, with working shells confirmed.**
   - Unit 42 has **retracted** its link between the European government intrusion and PHP2Shell: it is "unable to confirm that initial access is associated with the specific PHP2Shell CVEs." The intrusion itself (web shell, password hashes and MFA secrets, NTLM relay) still stands; its entry point is now unknown.
   - **CISA added both PHP2Shell CVEs (CVE-2026-109618 and -109619) to KEV on 26 Oct, due 29 Oct**, with forensic triage requirements.
   - A researcher's honeypots recorded **35 working shells** on 24 Oct (PHP 8.4.26, Apache mod_php, single worker, the most favourable case), using a chain that matches a public proof of concept.
   - Industrial vendors are now publishing advisories (Schneider Electric, Siemens RUGGEDCOM APE1808, ABB/B&R APROL, Johnson Controls, Belden NetModule with no patch).
   - **Action:** patch PHP to 8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35 and restart workers; treat unpatched internet-facing PHP as possibly compromised; hunt for wget, curl or php-cli spawned by httpd or php-fpm.
2. **The help desk is the front door: Falcon lists a second plaintiff law firm, and Arctic Wolf counts 400+ Teams "IT support" intrusions.**
   - **Beasley Allen**, a US plaintiff litigation firm, was listed by **Falcon** (26 Oct, ~214 GB claimed: settlement agreements, privileged memos, litigation strategy, trial transcripts). That is two mass-tort firms in two days after Weitz & Luxenberg.
   - Google and GuidePoint describe Falcon as a rebrand of **UNC6671 / BlackFile**: staff are phoned by fake IT help desks, sent to fake logins that steal session tokens, then the actor moves from Okta into M365 and Salesforce. GuidePoint counts 83+ victims including eight or nine law firms, and $8M+ paid. Since July the focus has been private equity, law firms and M&A and litigation data.
   - A plaintiff firm has already opened a **class-action investigation into White & Case** over Sunday's unverified Qilin listing.
   - **Arctic Wolf:** 400+ incidents where attackers used Microsoft Teams external chat, posing as IT support after an email bomb, to get users into Quick Assist or remote-management tools, sometimes ending in ransomware. Today's deep dive.
   - **Action:** brief staff that IT never cold-calls or cold-chats; require a callback to a known number before any remote session; block Teams chats from trial tenants and allow-list external domains.
3. **AI as the attacker goes mainstream.**
   - **South Korea:** police say one suspect used generative AI to breach about **90 organisations** and steal 8.2M records; AI-assisted activity in 60 of them, and 38 extorted.
   - **Cathedral**, a startup, says its AI agents reached Anthropic's sales, HR and IT data in **under 12 hours** in an authorised test, through flaws in third-party admin and sales SaaS. Anthropic says access was cut the same day and no customer prompts, model weights or code were touched.
   - **OpenAI** says its new GPT-6.1 Terra meets the "Critical" cyber threshold in its Preparedness Framework, and it is generally available at half the price of its predecessor.
   - The **testmetest** npm worm planted startup hooks for AI coding assistants (Claude, Gemini, Cursor, VS Code) in 42 GitHub repositories, with a wiper that fires if the stolen token is revoked.
   - **Action:** add AI-agent abuse to tabletop scenarios; inventory SaaS admin and sales tools as part of the attack surface; scan repos for unexpected assistant config files.

## 12. Threat actor deep dive: Microsoft Teams "IT help desk" intrusions (Arctic Wolf, 26 Oct; STAC4749 per Sophos)

### Summary
- Arctic Wolf's "Beyond the Inbox" report analyses **400+ incidents** in which attackers used Microsoft Teams **external chat**, on by default in most tenants, to impersonate IT support, usually after an email bomb, and talk users into Quick Assist or a remote-management tool. From there: payloads, LDAP reconnaissance, credential attacks, lateral movement, and sometimes ransomware.
- **Ransomware seen:** Chaos (via STAC4749), Black Basta, Cactus and 3AM; the Black Basta lineage also used QakBot, DarkGate and Cobalt Strike; a GoGRPC backdoor appeared.
- **Attribution:** activity aligns with **STAC4749**, Sophos's name for a financially motivated cluster that deployed Chaos ransomware against North American organisations from February to June 2026. A mix of affiliates, not a state actor.
- **Infrastructure shift:** throwaway `*.onmicrosoft.com` trial tenants now account for only 30–40% of cases; the rest use paid domains (about half .top, about 40% .com).
- **Why it matters to a consultancy:** we federate Teams heavily with clients, so staff are used to chatting with outsiders. That habit is exactly what this technique exploits.

### Timeline
- **2024–25:** Black Basta-linked email bombing plus Teams vishing.
- **Feb–Jun 2026:** STAC4749 campaign ending in Chaos ransomware (Quick Assist first; the RemSupp tool preferred from April; persistence retooled in May).
- **26 Oct 2026:** Arctic Wolf publishes its 400+ incident analysis.
- **Observed speed:** several users targeted within about 5 minutes; payload execution can follow **less than 15 minutes** after the user engages; Kerberoasting and Golden-Ticket-related alerts fired alongside the first Teams alert in some cases.

### Initial access
1. Email bomb floods the user with newsletter and subscription mail.
2. A one-to-one external Teams chat or call arrives from an IT-themed identity: "Help Desk", "IT Support", "IT Service Desk – Matt", "Enterprise IT & Security (Internal)", emoji, or the victim company's own name.
3. Vishing persuades the user to launch **Quick Assist** or install a remote-management tool.
4. The attacker does not paste links into Teams; they browse to the payload during the remote session, sidestepping Safe Links and ZAP.

### Tooling
- **Remote access:** Quick Assist, AnyDesk, ScreenConnect, TeamViewer, Atera, Zoho Assist, NetSupport, LogMeIn, plus **Level** and **RemSupp** for redundancy.
- **Arctic Wolf example chain:** PowerShell downloads an MSI from **Azure Blob Storage**; the MSI drops a PowerShell loader and a ZIP; the loader decrypts a second stage that extracts `pythonw` and the implant **`ss.py`**, which enumerates every AD user and computer with `[adsisearcher]` and sends results to randomly chosen C2 endpoints.
- **STAC4749 (Sophos):** loaders `sekv*.exe`, `helper*.exe`, `74fs*.exe`; a PyArmour-obfuscated PyInstaller backdoor (`updater.exe`, `msupdate.exe`) that fetches its AES key from a public GitHub repo and talks to `workers[.]dev` infrastructure; reverse SOCKS proxy `sc5.exe` to `legio[.]name`; Go implants with one-word names (`dizzy.exe`, `graph.exe`, `tube.exe`); DWAgent; Chaos ransomware (`readme.chaos.txt`).

### Persistence (STAC4749, per Sophos)
- HKCU Run keys disguised as Realtek audio ("Realtek HD Audio Universal Service", "Realtek Audio UHD") and, from May, "WinAudio life2".
- `mklink-*.vbs` scripts creating Startup shortcuts named like SecurityHealth or OneDriveUpdate.
- A second remote-management tool installed for redundancy; RDP enabled.

### IOCs (defanged)
- **Arctic Wolf tenants:** supportedge33.onmicrosoft[.]com, helpdeskteam365.onmicrosoft[.]com, itcoreportal.onmicrosoft[.]com, itendpoint365.onmicrosoft[.]com, itadmin922.onmicrosoft[.]com, corporateitservicellc.onmicrosoft[.]com
- **Arctic Wolf domains:** update-software[.]top, get-help[.]top, helpdesk-call[.]com, kilan[.]com[.]pl (full appendices in the source: ~11 tenants, ~25 .top domains, ~7 .com lookalikes)
- **Sophos STAC4749 vishing domains:** sequrityupdate[.]top, scan-security[.]top, system-online[.]top, system-connect[.]top, corp-connect[.]top, info-secure[.]top, supportsoft[.]top, update-syscontrol[.]top, sequpdate[.]top, service-clien[.]top, service-corporation[.]top, scansequrity[.]top, service-help[.]top, sequritycheck[.]top, it-service[.]top, upsecscan[.]top
- **Payload host:** fa5[.]flsdwnld[.]online
- **Loader C2:** 94[.]140[.]114[.]192:443, 94[.]140[.]115[.]18:443, 94[.]140[.]115[.]129:443
- **Pinned-certificate C2:** 193[.]29[.]57[.]37 (issuers loop-CA, connectify-CA, james-bond-CA)
- **Proxy C2:** legio[.]name
- **Host artefact:** C:\ProgramData\AppSreen\logs\appscreen.log
- Full Sophos list: STAC4749-Chaos_IOCs_July2026.csv on SophosLabs GitHub.

### Hunting
1. **Unified Audit Log:** `ChatCreated` events with external, trial or onmicrosoft tenants whose display names include help desk, IT, support or "(Internal)"; correlate with an email-bomb spike for the same user within about 30 minutes.
2. **Endpoint:** `QuickAssist.exe` or any listed remote tool starting shortly after an external Teams chat; `msiexec` installing from `*.blob.core.windows.net`; `pythonw.exe` from user-writable paths; PowerShell `[adsisearcher]` queries for all computers and persons.
3. **Persistence:** HKCU Run values named Realtek or WinAudio pointing into user profiles; new SecurityHealth or OneDriveUpdate Startup shortcuts; `mklink-*.vbs`.
4. **Identity:** bursts of RC4 Kerberos service-ticket requests (Kerberoasting) and Golden Ticket anomalies after a remote-assist session.
5. **Network:** newly registered .top domains; `workers.dev` beacons from non-browser processes; the IPs above.

### Response
- **Teams:** allow-list external domains; block trial tenants (`Set-CsTenantFederationConfiguration -ExternalAccessWithTrialTenants "Blocked"`); block unmanaged Teams accounts; enable external-sender warnings.
- **Remote access:** remove or block Quick Assist; allow only the sanctioned remote-management tool.
- **Process:** callback to a known help-desk number before any remote session; tell staff the real help desk never cold-contacts them through external Teams chat.
- **If engaged:** isolate the host; revoke sessions and tokens; reset credentials including possibly Kerberoasted service accounts; look for a second remote tool; if Golden Ticket alerts fired, rotate KRBTGT twice.
