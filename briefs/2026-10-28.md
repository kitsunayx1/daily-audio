## 1. Top three things to know

1. **Marketing and analytics SaaS is the soft underbelly: one Japanese email vendor leaked 51.6M records across 49 client systems.**
   - **Yumiru Link's "Cuenote FC"** email-delivery service: an attacker exploited a vulnerability, stole a support staff member's credentials, planted malware and exported client databases. Second report (26 Oct): **51,628,280 records** from 49 systems (a cumulative count, not unique people), mostly email addresses plus member IDs and names. Named clients include JR Tokai, JR West, JR Kyushu, Resona Bank, Nomura Securities, Cybozu, Mainichi Shimbun and Saitama Prefecture; Bandai Namco (346,247 records) and NTT PC Communications disclosed on 27 Oct. **Phishing impersonating Saison Card was then sent from Cuenote's own trusted domain.**
   - **Amplitude** (product analytics): Coinbase says an Amplitude incident exposed email addresses and phone numbers for a "small subset" of its customers, and that other Amplitude customers were affected too. SlowMist claims 70,000+ Coinbase users (unconfirmed). No Amplitude statement found.
   - Also in the window: **ENGIE** (French customers' data via a partner marketing platform), **RMS Cloud** (Australian holiday-park booking SaaS; guests getting WhatsApp and SMS scams quoting real bookings), **Warp** (AI agent logs and telemetry via an unnamed vendor, about 1% of users), and Cisco Talos on scams sent through **Staples' and Dell's genuine order-notification systems**, passing DKIM and DMARC.
   - **Action:** list every marketing, email-delivery, analytics and booking platform that holds personal data or sends mail in the firm's name, and confirm which ones we or our clients use (check Cuenote, Amplitude, RMS). Expect phishing from legitimate-looking sending domains.
2. **CISA puts the Brotli flaw and a Magento upload bug on KEV; PHP2Shell's deadline is tomorrow.**
   - **CVE-2026-111497 (Brotli, "Node2Shell")** and **CVE-2026-48356 (Adobe Commerce / Magento file upload, CVSS 9.3)** added 27 Oct, due 10 Nov. Brotli is now officially exploited; VMware's SDDC Manager is affected with **no patch yet**.
   - **PHP2Shell** federal deadline is **29 Oct**.
   - **YOOtheme Pro** (WordPress theme) CVE-2026-102785 is exploited in the wild, not yet in KEV.
   - **Tailscale fixed "Tailworm"** in v1.104.4; headscale is still unpatched. **Veeam** (Backup & Replication, CVSS 9.1) and **WatchGuard** (seven critical) also patched.
   - **Action:** apply Adobe's July Commerce patch to every store, including microsites; inventory Brotli in containers and appliances and apply VMware's workaround; patch Veeam backup servers first.
3. **Election infrastructure: the FEC's filing system went offline after an attempted breach, six days before the midterms.**
   - Late Monday the US **Federal Election Commission** took e-filing offline. An FEC email to compliance firms said: "We have a bad actor attempting to breach FEC systems." No successful breach confirmed; no actor named; voting unaffected. Committees cannot file 24- and 48-hour spending reports.
   - **Israel's vote** passed without reported cyber disruption. Handala claimed a physical "bouquet" stunt at polling sites and against opposition candidate Gadi Eisenkot (unverified, no threat found). INSS says the post-poll period is the most sensitive.
   - **Whitearmor** escalated from J.B. Hunt to **Ryder** ($20M demanded, 18.75M rows claimed) and **Echo Global Logistics** ($10M).
   - **Action:** brief public-affairs and political-compliance client teams; expect "FEC filing restored, re-submit here" lures (our assessment, not observed).

## 6. AI attacks, breaches and trends

## 12. Threat actor deep dive: compromised camera-management software (Huntress, 27 Oct)

### Summary
- In several customer environments in September and October, a camera management system (`CMS.exe` under `C:\Program Files (x86)\CMS\`) spawned a reverse shell, which then sideloaded beaconing DLLs through a copied, legitimate Windows binary.
- **Vendor unconfirmed.** Strings point to a "CMS" package on kvision[.]ai and to 3r-dataware[.]com, which Huntress links to Korean firm 3R Global; Huntress calls this a possible source only.
- **Attribution:** none.
- **Why it matters:** video-management clients sit on facilities PCs and security desks in offices everywhere, are rarely inventoried by IT, and usually run with broad network reach.

### Timeline
- **Sep–Oct 2026:** incidents across several Huntress customers.
- **27 Oct 2026:** published by Harlan Carvey and Lindsey O'Donnell-Welch.

### Initial access
- The camera management software itself was compromised; how is unknown because the application kept no logs. A trojanised installer, a poisoned update channel or a remote feature of the app are all possible; none is confirmed.

### Execution chain
1. `CMS.exe` launches `rs.win32.exe` from `%APPDATA%\CMS\RemoteSetup\`, passing a remote IP, port 8000 and a hardcoded "admin" username and password. Huntress assesses it as a reverse shell.
2. `rs.win32.exe` launches `WpcMon.exe` (Windows Parental Controls), copied to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Microsoft\WpcMon\`.
3. `WpcMon.exe` sideloads `IPHLPAPI.DLL` and `samcli.dll`, modified zlib builds with beaconing added.
4. C2: 144.172.116[.]37, seen in every incident.

### Persistence
- An HKCU `Run` value pointing to the copied `WpcMon.exe`, so the payload starts at every sign-in.

### IOCs (defanged)
- SHA-256 `rs.win32.exe`: 1fb4af4862643dcab1459872f723f574f1a588855b020916b600df5c20681144
- SHA-256 `samcli.dll`: 8f8e88c54a9c56174974711eff99b4beb79536642fcca1902acdc3d4b1fd1035
- SHA-256 `IPHLPAPI.DLL`: 70b2345302264c41504d59e6a63d6d908969740424b42b7373f10b4c8d74c87a
- C2: 144.172.116[.]37
- Domains in `CMS.exe` strings (not confirmed as C2): 3r-dataware[.]com, cloudapi.3r-dataware[.]com; possible download source kvision[.]ai
- Paths: `C:\Users\<user>\AppData\Roaming\CMS\RemoteSetup\`, `C:\Users\<user>\AppData\Local\Microsoft\WpcMon\`
- String: `developerslavery` (embedded in `CMS.exe`)

### Hunting
1. Process tree `CMS.exe` → `rs.win32.exe` → `WpcMon.exe`.
2. `WpcMon.exe` running from anywhere other than `C:\Windows\System32`.
3. `IPHLPAPI.DLL` or `samcli.dll` loaded from user-writable paths.
4. HKCU Run values pointing into AppData.
5. Outbound connections to 144.172.116[.]37, or to port 8000 from camera or VMS hosts.
6. Hosts running "CMS" camera software from unidentified vendors, especially ones referencing kvision or 3r-dataware.

### Response
- Inventory video-management and CCTV clients, including those run by facilities teams and building landlords.
- Isolate affected hosts, block the IP, remove the Run key and files, then reimage; rotate credentials used on those hosts.
- Block execution from AppData with WDAC or AppLocker.
- Put physical-security systems on their own network segment. (Related: CERT-UA's Dahua warning in section 4.)

### MITRE ATT&CK (mapping by our researcher; Huntress gives none)
T1195.002 (suspected), T1059, T1574.002, T1036.005, T1547.001, T1071.001, T1571, T1105

### Sources
- https://www.huntress.com/blog/camera-management-software-compromise-malware
