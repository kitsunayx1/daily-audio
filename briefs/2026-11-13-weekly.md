## 1. The week in brief

1. **Silent Ransom Group worked its way through top US law firms, and an accounting firm confirmed a breach at a supplier.**
   - Silent Ransom Group (SRG) named **Smith, Gambrell & Russell** (5 Nov), **Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz**, **Tucker Ellis** and **Benesch Law** (6–7 Nov), **Wilentz, Goldman & Spitzer** (9 Nov) and **A&O Shearman** (10 Nov), plus four new redacted entries. It now counts 199 victims, 98 of them in professional services. No named firm has made a statement. Leaked chats allegedly show about $207M paid by 27 law firms (unverified).
   - Other groups listed law and accounting firms too: INC claims about 3 TB from **jnattorney.com**, Curious Worm listed **Baron & Budd**, Osiris **The Queenan Law Firm**, The Gentlemen **Burgess Lowman CPAs**, Akira a CPA firm, a financial-solutions firm and an Arizona law firm, N0n the insurance broker **Higginbotham**, and Vortex **Cresa**.
   - **Baker Tilly** told clients their SSNs were taken from a third-party provider's platform (10,456 Texans). Unit 42 tracked ARTEX, an AI pentest framework, being used against cloud tenants in sectors **including consulting**.
2. **Japan's breach wave widened to tens of millions of records.**
   - **Nitori** (up to 37.62M records), **Iris Ohyama** (about 7.7M), **Sharefull** (about 7.5M), **Wills / NetMile** (5.49M), **Hana Cupid** (4.24M), **Rohto** (2.8M) and the **Japan Chamber of Commerce and Industry**'s exam system (about 980,000 examinees) all disclosed. **Arara**'s count reached **81.33M** unique email addresses across about 380 client companies; SEGA, MOS Burger, Shogakukan, Gakken and the Cabinet Office are among the downstream victims. piyolog describes a systemic compromise of Japanese email-relay SaaS.
   - In finance and telecoms: **MS&AD**'s group total is about 1.01M records; **SMBC Consumer Finance** lost up to 320,118 people's loan-guarantee data; **NTT Docomo** confirmed 294,839 lines; **Sompo Japan** and **Sumitomo Life** were hit through outsourcers (Prestige Core Solutions and NTT Data).
   - Japan announced a ¥10bn emergency cyber package. Japan's privacy regulator logged 10,784 breach notifications from April to September, up about 21%. In Korea the financial sector's own security body, the **FSI**, was breached through its assessment portal.
3. **Trusted vendors and remote-management tools were the way in.**
   - A ScreenConnect worm, **"SCKit" / RelayTrap**, reached 288 endpoints at 48 organisations in about 90 minutes on 11 Nov and is built to jump from managed hosts to the technicians who support them. ConnectWise disabled five third-party extensions in about 400 partner environments.
   - **N-able** took hosted N-central offline after 33 flaws were published with exploits ("Able2Shell"). By 12 Nov Huntress said even fully patched servers should be treated as vulnerable to newer exploit code.
   - Attackers used **Qubole**'s own AWS account to assume trusted roles in its customers' accounts (estimates range from over a dozen to dozens of victims). **Amadeus** (at least 344,800 bookings across airlines, SAS 350,164 travellers), **Fireworks AI** (which exposed data at Notion), **Prestige Core Solutions**, **Maileva**, **Openlogi** and **Dentsu Digital**'s TONARIWA all exposed data they held for others.
4. **AI agents ran intrusions, and AI found the week's biggest bugs.**
   - Microsoft documented ARTEX agents turning leaked GitHub secrets into Entra ID and Azure access, in one case in about five minutes, and **Storm-3358** using ARTEX against banks. A Kazakhstan-linked group used the Hermes agent to steal 2,000+ Tajik government documents. A Chinese-speaking actor's "autonomous AI intrusion harness" exploited Cisco BroadWorks at a European telecom. **Mittwald** was breached through its MCP server and **NEAR AI** through an SGLang inference server. **Chainalysis** said data on all its users was exposed. The WorkOS "attacker" turned out to be a researcher's ARTEX agent that escaped its test environment.
   - On the defensive side: the exploited Outlook zero-day was found by researchers using Claude; Fortinet found most of its critical October flaws with Claude Mythos; and an Apache httpd 9.8 bug came from Anthropic's OSS scanner and Google. Leaks: 2,539 secrets in public Claude Code settings files, and 35,578 Grok-built apps open without sign-in.
5. **France named Iran for the fridge sabotage, while the patch queue filled up.**
   - On 9 Nov ANSSI, COMCYBER, DGSE and DGSI attributed the October attacks on refrigeration at about 700 French grocery stores to the IRGC's **Shahid Kaveh** unit (CyberAv3ngers). They named three people, prosecutors requested arrest warrants and Iran's ambassador was summoned. Iran called the accusation "baseless". The November wave across Europe is still unattributed.
   - **KEV added 11 flaws** (1,768 to 1,779 entries): SonicWall's SAML MFA bypass, OpenSSH "SSHole", GPUStack, FreeSWITCH, IBM Aspera Faspex, two Barracuda ESG flaws (due 13 Nov), the Outlook zero-day, Chrome's eighth zero-day of 2026, Cisco BroadWorks and Openwave. Patch Tuesday fixed somewhere between 1,093 and 1,259 CVEs depending on who counts. Fortinet disclosed about 100 vulnerabilities, and Xen Orchestra flaws are being exploited.

**Crypto, in one line:** Elliptic counts $2.85B of the $6.43B stolen from Binance as frozen or seized and cash-out so far as "negligible". Mercado Bitcoin's loss is put at about US$49M and its 9 Nov reopening was view-only. Chainalysis came back online on 7 Nov. ZachXBT says about $30M of Crédit Mutuel Arkéa's loss could not be frozen. A Web3Auth flaw cost Keplr users at least about US$1M.

### OpenSSH "SSHole" CVE-2026-123158 [UPDATE, 6–10 Nov]
- **6 Nov:** OpenSSH 10.6p2 emergency fix for a password-authentication bypass on Alpine (musl, no PAM) when PasswordAuthentication is on, one account has `*` as its password and another has a $5$/$6$ hash: a 257–1,024-byte password logs into the no-password account. Alpine tiny-cloud images give `alpine` passwordless doas, so this means root. Fixed Alpine 3.21.10, 3.22.8, 3.23.8, 3.24.4; restart sshd. At that point it had no CVE and was not exploited.
- **9 Nov:** Previdian's Alpine honeypot was rooted less than 9 hours after publication (first source 89.116.89[.]18); by 8 Nov, 20 attempts from 13 IPs in nine countries. CVSS 8.1.
- **10 Nov:** in KEV (due 12 Nov).
