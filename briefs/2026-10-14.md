## 1. Top three things to know

1. **Ivanti Connect Secure zero-day chain (CVE-2026-104935) is being exploited, and one link in the chain is still unpatched.**
   - Volexity says a new actor, UTA0576, has chained three bugs since about 1–3 Oct to get unauthenticated root on *fully patched* VPN appliances.
   - The actor patches the appliance only in memory, so Ivanti's Integrity Checker misses it. It then steals LDAP credentials, LAPS passwords and BitLocker keys.
   - CISA added the CVE to KEV on 13 Oct with a federal deadline of 16 Oct. That deadline covers both patching and forensic triage.
   - About 5,500 instances are exposed (Shadowserver).
   - **Action:** patch, then hunt for compromise. Do not rely on the Integrity Checker.
2. **Microsoft's record Patch Tuesday (about 1,400 CVEs) includes an exploited Mark-of-the-Web bypass (CVE-2026-96951).**
   - It is in KEV with a deadline of 16 Oct.
   - It is a phishing enabler: a crafted attachment skips SmartScreen and Protected View.
   - Also notable: a zero-click Outlook Reading Pane RCE (CVSS 9.8), plus Exchange, AD and SharePoint fixes.
   - **Action:** triage by exploitation status, not by volume.
3. **Insider and hiring threats to knowledge firms.**
   - **OpenAI:** an engineer was arrested after a hidden recorder was found in a conference room.
   - **DPRK IT workers:** DOJ's "Operation Lonestar Roundup" dismantled North Korean IT-worker laptop farms across Texas (more than 70 US employers, about $3M).
   - **Kimsuky:** posing as conference organisers and policy researchers, the same pretexts consultants and MGI staff receive.
   - **Action:** sweep meeting rooms used for sensitive client work, check identity and the laptop shipping address when onboarding remote contractors, and verify speaker invitations through a separate channel.

  - an LLM user (medium confidence);
  - preparing for lateral movement when it was disrupted (high confidence).
- **Timeline:**
  - 30 Sep–7 Oct: an OpenVPN server ran on the attacker's VPS.
  - 1–3 Oct: exploitation began (sources differ on the exact date).
  - 5 Oct: Volexity started investigating.
  - 13 Oct: public disclosure and patch.
- **TTPs:**
  - **Payload:** Perl disguised as a ZIP and run before wu.cgi deletes it. It loads Python in memory and patches libdsplibs.so in memory to get root.
  - **No persistence:** no web shell. Commands come in over the exploit's own HTTPS.
  - **Credential and AD theft:** stole stored LDAP credentials and exported AD as JSON. A custom LDAP tool hunted LAPS passwords, BitLocker keys and userPassword attributes. A custom Kerberos tool did Kerberoasting (T1558.003).
  - **Other activity:** password spraying (T1110.003) and tcpdump renamed `dsstatmond` to sniff LDAP, Basic and NTLM credentials.
  - **Anti-forensics:** selective log edits, then restoring memory and DSCache.
- **IOCs:**
  - IP: 108.165.147[.]64 (Evoxt VPS)
  - Requests: POST `/dana-na/auth/url_default/welcome.cgi` with `X-DSCGI-PATH: /home/webserver/htdocs/dana/fb/smb/wu.cgi` or `/home/runtime/tmp/<md5>`
  - Files:
    - `/tmp/ml.py`, `rq5.py`, `mk.py`, `rr.py`, `sp_run.py`, `pu.txt`, `sp_out.txt`, `spray_hits.json`
    - `/data/.icscap/h.py`, `/data/.icscap/dsstatmond`, `/data/.icscap/cap*`
    - `/home/runtime/tmp/ics-root-run-inband-*.pl`
  - Process: `dsstatmond`
  - Hashes are on Volexity's GitHub.
- **Actions:**
  - Patch.
  - Run memory and network forensics, not the Integrity Checker.
  - Treat traffic originating from the appliance as suspicious.
  - Rotate LDAP service accounts, LAPS passwords and BitLocker keys if exposed.
