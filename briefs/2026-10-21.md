## 1. Top three things to know

1. **Ransomware crews are coming through the VPN, and professional and legal services are on the target list.**
   - Unit 42 says **Qilin and INC** are exploiting the Palo Alto GlobalProtect / Prisma Access flaw CVE-2026-0257 to get VPN sessions without credentials or MFA. Victims include professional and legal services.
   - After getting in, they pivot to vCenter and ESXi and dump domain credentials from the domain controller's virtual disk. That bypasses EDR inside the guest. They also abuse Veeam CVE-2023-27532.
   - Separately, the FBI, CISA, the Secret Service and German and UK partners issued a joint advisory on **SafePay**: more than 600 victims since August 2024, entry through Cisco ASA, SonicWall and Veeam flaws plus password guessing. Deep dive in section 12.
   - **Action:** ask IT whether GlobalProtect uses authentication-override cookies and whether the cookie certificate is reused. Confirm the fixed PAN-OS versions, and confirm Veeam, Cisco ASA and SonicWall are patched.
2. **A busy day for the software supply chain.**
   - **ConnectWise [UPDATE]:** about 1,000 Entra tenants saw sign-in attempts, and more than 300 appear to have succeeded (Huntress, via CyberScoop). ConnectWise has reset credentials; there is still no restoration date.
   - **OVHcloud CLI:** five GitHub releases were swapped for a secret stealer on 19 Oct.
   - **UpdraftCentral:** stolen connection keys from TeamUpdraft were used to plant backdoor plugins on WordPress sites. Roughly 400,000 sites could be exposed.
   - **eMudhra:** eight intermediate CA private keys were recoverable. Browsers have blocked them, which may break some legitimate sites.
   - **Action:** check marketing WordPress sites for UpdraftCentral keys, and ask developers whether anyone ran the OVHcloud CLI.
3. **PHP emergency patches land today, and a FortiSIEM exploit is public.**
   - Two critical PHP remote code execution flaws ("PHP2Shell", CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) leaked before fixes. Patches are due at 14:00 UTC today for PHP 8.2 to 8.5 only. Older branches get nothing.
   - No exploitation is known yet. SecurityWeek's headline: "Mass Exploitation Looms."
   - Horizon3.ai has published a proof of concept for FortiSIEM CVE-2026-106173 (unauthenticated RCE, fixed in 7.5.1).
   - **Action:** inventory PHP, including inside appliances. Patch internet-facing PHP first and isolate end-of-life versions.

### Palo Alto GlobalProtect / Prisma Access CVE-2026-0257: Qilin and INC [OLDER CVE, new reporting]
- **The flaw:** authentication-override cookies are decrypted and trusted without a signature check. Only exposed if override cookies are enabled **and** the cookie certificate is reused (for example as the portal or gateway HTTPS certificate). Disclosed 13 May; exploited since 17 May.
- **New from Unit 42 (19–20 Oct):** INC ransomware is now named alongside Qilin. Sectors include professional and legal services. In one case the attackers went from the VPN to vCenter, turned off `execInstalledOnly` on ESXi hosts and dumped credentials offline from a domain controller's virtual disk. Chained with Veeam CVE-2023-27532.
- **Fixed versions:**

  | Branch | Fixed releases |
  |---|---|
  | PAN-OS 12.1 | 12.1.4-h6, 12.1.7 |
  | PAN-OS 11.2 | 11.2.4-h17, 11.2.7-h14, 11.2.10-h7, 11.2.12 |
  | PAN-OS 11.1 | 11.1.4-h33, 11.1.6-h32, 11.1.7-h6, 11.1.10-h25, 11.1.13-h5, 11.1.15 |
  | PAN-OS 10.2 | 10.2.7-h34, 10.2.10-h36, 10.2.13-h21, 10.2.16-h7, 10.2.18-h6 |
  | Prisma Access | 11.2.7-h13; 10.2.10-h36 |

- **Mitigation:** disable authentication override, or give the cookies their own certificate. Kill all GlobalProtect sessions after patching.
- **IOCs:**

## 6. AI attacks, breaches and trends

### The "AI agent hacker" story has a name: Cairn [UPDATE]
- NorForge's Snorre Fagerland found about two dozen exposed panels of **Cairn**, an open-source multi-agent pentest engine, being used by three criminal clusters since about 2 Oct.
- The largest, Chinese-language cluster ran more than 1,100 attack projects against e-commerce and payment sites in the US, Japan and Australia, aiming for persistent webshells.
- About 10% succeeded, at roughly $50 of compute per successful hack and just under 23 hours on average. Reuters says more than 100 companies were breached, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins.
- Hundreds of secrets were in the panel data; 11% were still valid.
- **Indicators:** exposed Cairn panels (often port 8000, no login) and hidden upload forms in web roots.

## 12. Threat actor deep dive: SafePay (joint advisory AA26-293A, 20 Oct)

**Source note:** cisa.gov was unreachable. Everything below comes from secondary reporting on the advisory, mainly Cyber Security News, which reproduces its IOC tables. Re-verify against the PDF before blocking.

### Summary
- **Authors:** CISA, FBI and the US Secret Service, with Germany's LKA Rheinland-Pfalz, the UK National Crime Agency and the North West Regional Organised Crime Unit.
- **Scale:** more than 600 victims in North America, South America, Europe and Australia since August 2024. The advisory covers activity from November 2024 to August 2026.
- **Model:** a private group with no affiliates.
- **Extortion:** double extortion with a Tor leak site, pressure phone calls, and demands set as a share of the victim's revenue.
- **Named incidents (The Record):** Ingram Micro, Microlise and Conduent.

### Initial access
- Stolen or exposed credentials, brute force and phished accounts.
- VPN and RDP access, including from Mullvad VPN exit addresses (commercial VPN, not a Mullvad compromise).
- Automated exploitation of edge and backup systems:
  - Cisco ASA/FTD: CVE-2020-3259 and CVE-2023-20269
  - Veeam Backup & Replication: CVE-2023-27532 and CVE-2024-40711
  - SonicWall SonicOS: CVE-2024-40766

### Tooling
- **Lateral movement:** PsExec, WinRM, RDP; AnyDesk and TeamViewer used to copy the ransomware.
- **Discovery:** ShareFinder.ps1, SharpShares.exe, SoftPerfect netscan.exe, NetExec (nxc.exe), nltest.
- **Credentials:** Mimikatz, dcsync.exe, UAC bypass, token impersonation.
- **Tunnelling:** Socks.exe and Socks_werfault.exe (a SOCKS proxy disguised as a Windows process).
- **Exfiltration:** 7-Zip and WinRAR staging; RClone, WinSCP, FileZilla, OneDrive or Backblaze B2 (b2.exe).
- **Encryption:** locker.dll or 1.exe; ChaCha20 plus RSA; extension `.SafePay`; ransom note points to a Tor chat portal. The binary may delete itself.

### Persistence and impact
- Creates new admin accounts or takes over existing ones, and changes users' passwords to lock staff out.
- Disables security software.
- Deletes Volume Shadow Copies and factory-resets NAS backup devices.

### IOCs (from the advisory tables as reproduced; defanged)

Treat these as leads that need corroboration.

### Hunting
1. **VPN and edge:** successful VPN or RDP-gateway logins from commercial VPN ranges; spray-then-success patterns; host names of the form `WIN-` plus 11 random characters. Confirm Cisco ASA/FTD, SonicWall and Veeam are patched.
2. **Identity:** events 4720 (account created), 4728/4732 (added to privileged group), bulk 4724 (password resets); DCSync from non-domain controllers.
3. **Execution:** event 7045 (new service, e.g. PSEXESVC); WinRM or RDP fan-out from one host; AnyDesk or TeamViewer on servers; nxc, netscan, SharpShares or ShareFinder running.
4. **Exfiltration:** rclone, WinSCP or b2.exe; large archives in temp folders; uploads to personal OneDrive or Backblaze.
5. **Pre-impact:** shadow-copy deletion; NAS or backup-appliance resets; EDR tampering; `.SafePay` files.
6. **Overlap:** SafePay and Qilin both abuse Veeam CVE-2023-27532, so one backup-server hunt covers both.

### Response
- Isolate hosts and preserve logs. Rotate domain admin, service and backup credentials, and reset KRBTGT twice.
- Remove unsanctioned remote tools. Restrict PsExec, WinRM and RDP to privileged access workstations.
- Require phishing-resistant MFA on every remote entry point.
- Keep at least one offline or immutable backup with separate credentials, and segment backup and NAS management.
