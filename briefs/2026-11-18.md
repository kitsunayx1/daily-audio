## 12. Deep dive: COLD RELIC (COLDRIVER / Star Blizzard) turns one cloud compromise into the next [NEW]

**Primary:** Mandiant/GTIG, "COLD RELIC: A Case Study in Impersonation and Compromise", 17 Nov 2026 (time of day not shown; may fall just before the window). https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/cold-relic-a-case-study-in-impersonation-and-compromise
**Secondary:** https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/russian-cold-relic-hackers-use-stolen.html

### Why it matters for a global consulting firm
- COLD RELIC uses data stolen from one organisation (directories, mail, relationships) to impersonate its staff to trusted partners in the same professional circles: a consulting firm's client and alliance network.
- A consultancy is both springboard (client rosters, contacts, CVs, event schedules) and target of approaches that "come from" a compromised client.
- Identity-only, cross-cloud (Google Workspace plus Entra ID SSO), no malware: EDR will not see it; identity and SaaS logs will.
- New COLD RELIC phishing seen as recently as November 2026.

### Attribution and confidence
- GTIG: "Russian state-sponsored threat group" (formerly COLDRIVER, UNC4057; also Star Blizzard, Callisto, Gossamer Bear); cites the DOJ assessment that it "operates within Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB)". No explicit confidence level for these intrusions. The case link rests on phishing-URL parameters and pretexts naming the first victim.

### Timeline
- Late 2025: Case 1, a US organisation's Workspace tenant compromised via a Super Admin account for "several months".
- After eviction: impersonation of Case 1 staff against internal staff and associates.
- June 2026: a redirector URL naming the Case 1 victim submitted to VirusTotal.
- 2026: Case 2, a second US organisation (Entra ID federated SSO for Workspace) compromised via impersonation of a Case 1 employee.
- November 2026: new phishing operations observed.

### Initial access
- Case 1: stolen Super Admin credentials plus MFA push fatigue, through a US residential proxy near the victim (defeats impossible-travel).
- Case 2: contact on an encrypted messaging app posing as a Case 1 employee; phishing links captured Entra credentials and tokens, then redirected to a relevant lure document.
- Hallmark: a compromised website as first link, fingerprinting then redirecting; URL parameters naming a prior victim or lure (`?file=`, `?ref=`).

### Attack chain
1. Push-fatigue acceptance; new device model registered.
2. Persistence via passkeys, MFA backup codes and rogue MFA devices; passkeys survived password and session resets.
3. Collection: whole-organisation Drive exfiltration by mass-sharing to an attacker mailbox and direct download; tenant-wide **Customer Takeout**; employee list export.
4. Counter-forensics: queried security-investigation and email audit logs; viewed, modified and deleted **Alert Center** notifications.
5. Long-term access: OAuth mailbox sync to external clients; searches for credentials, .pst, personnel data.
6. After eviction: impersonation of the victim's staff to re-phish.
7. Case 2: new MFA device in SSO; bulk download; messaging profile renamed to a new persona.
8. A failed remediation: passwords reset but sessions not revoked, so access continued.

### IOCs
- **None published** (no IPs, domains or hashes). Example residential-proxy ASNs AS5650 and AS20115 are consumer ISPs, not attacker infrastructure: do not block blindly.
- Behavioural: phishing URLs on compromised sites carrying victim or lure-name parameters.

### Hunting (GTIG table; Workspace event names as printed)
- MFA spam then success from the same IP (T1621).
- OAuth `Authorize` granting `https://mail.google.com/` to unapproved clients such as Proton or Thunderbird (T1114.002, T1528).
- `CUSTOMER_TAKEOUT_CREATED` (T1119).
- Drive `Download` spikes by one user from residential ASNs (T1530); external-sharing spikes, especially to @gmail.com (T1567.002).
- `Passkey enrolled` / `Device Registered` after a fatigue pattern (T1098.005); login challenge type `Backup code` from non-corporate ASNs (T1078.004).
- `Batch Delete Operation Performed` on Alert Center alerts (T1562.008); `User List Download` (T1087.004); unexpected `Investigation Query` (T1082); Drive searches for .pst, .bak, password, cred (T1083, T1552.001).
- Entra equivalents (ours): new MFA method registrations; SSO sign-ins from residential ASNs in the user's own city.

### Response (GTIG)
- FIDO2/WebAuthn hardware keys; consider Advanced Protection.
- Restrict passkey and MFA-device registration (admin or out-of-band approval).
- On compromise: revoke all sessions, refresh tokens and OAuth grants in both Workspace and Entra; remove rogue passkeys, backup codes and MFA devices. A password reset alone is not enough.
- Review OAuth grants; flag desktop/external mail clients; enrich sign-ins with residential-proxy reputation; restrict admin from consumer ISPs; DLP on rosters and .pst; disable Takeout where not needed.
- Ours: verify any client or partner who reaches out on Signal or WhatsApp with a document link through a known channel; brief partner-facing staff that a compromised client can be the lure.
