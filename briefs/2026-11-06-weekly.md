## 1. The week in brief

1. **Law and accounting firms were hit through the help desk, and the leak-site listings kept coming.**
   - Four Bay Street firms are now confirmed breached: **McCarthy Tétrault**, **Davies**, **Bennett Jones** and **BLG**. At least 7 employees gave access after Teams messages and spoofed calls from fake "IT". Canada's Cyber Centre (AL26-028) issued a warning about help-desk vishing against legal and financial firms, and Microsoft documented the same pretext leading to AD CS abuse and DCSync.
   - **Silent Ransom Group** listed **Susman Godfrey**, **ArentFox Schiff** and **Moritt Hock & Hamroff**, and posted six new redacted entries; **Falcon** claims 260 GB from **Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney**; a new group, **Soul**, posted two Birmingham law firms and an NJ accounting firm in one batch; CPA and tax firms (INTAX, Krishnan Company, hhjcpa.com) were listed too.
   - **PwC:** a forum post claims 260 GB of Azure data and GitHub repositories, separate from Everest's claim (snippet only). **Accenture** disputes a WIRED report that a contractor's personal AI agent ran on a Pentagon network.
2. **Suppliers leaked their customers' data at scale, and one bank lost about €118M through a provider.**
   - Japan's **Arara** mail-delivery breach (about 77M addresses across about 380 client environments) spilled into **TKC**'s accounting and local-government software, with Asoview (15.8M), Bic Camera group (14.9M), Yokohama, Osaka and many more downstream.
   - **Douzone Bizon** (Korean ERP backup, 27 organisations named, regulator investigating), **Securitize Japan / NTT Data** (digital bonds), **Amadeus** (more airlines and NH Travel), **Daou Office** (a zero-day behind Bithumb and Hanyang Securities) and **cPGuard** (a hijacked update channel that reached about 2,950 servers) all exposed data held for others.
   - **Crédit Mutuel Arkéa** confirmed attackers used an unnamed service provider's platform to move about €118M (US$133M) out of its own account; **MS&AD**'s two big insurers halted customer services after an intrusion.
3. **AI agents ran intrusions, and AI developer platforms were the target.**
   - **ARTEX** surfaced in WorkOS's breach (named by its CEO), in Kernel's cryptomining incident, possibly at Chainstack, in CrowdStrike's VAPOR PANDA campaign against 200+ organisations, and in a backdoored Docker image; **GitBook** called its outage "an attack driven largely by automation and AI"; Chainalysis reported "offensive agentic techniques".
   - Kaspersky (CyberStrikeAI), Sophos (Aur0ra using coding agents), Acronis (one operator running 12 agents) and Talos (VOLTA asks an LLM what each victim is worth) all documented AI in real intrusions.
   - A trojanised **"Claude Code" extension on the Cursor Marketplace** planted SSH backdoors on up to 5,000 developer machines; SecurityWeek counts about 13 AI developer-platform breaches in a month.
4. **North Korea stole about $6.3B from Binance, the largest crypto theft on record.**
   - Hot wallets on ten chains were drained on 29 Oct; the FBI attributed it to **TraderTraitor** on 31 Oct and published 628 addresses. About $2.85B is frozen; laundering continues through Tron, THORChain and other bridges.
   - Binance's post-mortem: malware on one engineer's machine in June and a fake job interview for a second in September, then abuse of an internal wallet signing service ([CONFLICT] on the exact lure).
   - Brazil's **Mercado Bitcoin** lost about US$37–49M on 2 Nov; Polymarket traders lost about $2.7M through a hijacked DebugBear script.
5. **Attacks reached the physical world, and edge devices kept breaking.**
   - Supermarket **refrigeration controllers** were tampered with across at least a dozen European countries, the UK included, from 1–2 Nov, after French incidents in October and US military commissary freezer failures in August; a hacktivist claim by NoName057(16) and Z-Pentest is widely doubted.
   - Ukraine's and Russia's **emergency-alert apps** were hijacked (Zelensky confirmed Ukraine's retaliation); Belarusian Cyber Partisans claim a destructive attack on Rosstat.
   - **Ivanti Connect Secure** had two more zero-days chained by an actor that leaves no implant; KEV added GitLab, Cisco ASA/FTD, Ivanti, WordPress core and an Apple flaw used in **APT29**'s watering-hole campaign against iPhones and Android.

**Elections, in one line:** the US midterms on 3 Nov passed without a significant cyber incident: failed login attempts, traffic spikes, smishing and bomb-threat hoaxes, no foreign-influence reports to EI-ISAC by Tuesday night, and no CISA election-day briefings; Democrats won both chambers.

## 12. Actor deep dives this week

- **TraderTraitor (DPRK) and the Binance theft (TRM and others), 30 Oct:**
  - From 20:05 UTC on 29 Oct, hot wallets on ten chains sent out about $6.36B (TRM) within about 20 minutes, with a second wave on Avalanche and Polygon at 23:05. One transaction submitted **50 pre-signed EIP-7702 authorisations** tied to consecutive nonces, turning the Ethereum hot wallet into a sweeper; three small Tornado Cash withdrawals funded the operator wallet on 28 Oct and attack code was rehearsed on test wallets. TRM's assessment: the attacker gained the ability to make Binance's **hot-wallet signing system** sign transactions of its choosing (signatures across chains and key types within about 30 minutes, which phishing does not explain). Response: rotate credentials for engineers who can reach signing or payment paths, review container exec and audit logs from 26 Oct, separate signing infrastructure by system and value tier.
