## 12. Threat actor deep dive: UAT-11473 and "KeyDrift" in Cisco SD-WAN controllers (Cisco Talos, 28 Oct)

### Summary
- UAT-11473 exploited Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller CVE-2026-20127 (auth bypass, CVSS 10, in KEV since Feb 2026) and implanted **KeyDrift**, a custom framework living inside the control-plane process `vdaemon` on vSmart and vBond. It intercepts decrypted control traffic and steals keying material, including (Talos assesses) OMP-distributed AES-256 IPsec keys, giving the potential to decrypt or redirect an enterprise's SD-WAN overlay. Development is ongoing.
- **Victims:** space and satellite services, defence and aerospace technology, and policy research. No names or geography.
- **Why it matters:** SD-WAN controllers sit at the centre of branch and office connectivity for large enterprises; whoever holds the keys can read the overlay.

### Attribution and confidence
- Talos assesses with **high confidence** that UAT-11473's infrastructure overlaps considerably with Volt Typhoon's "Silent Train" network (Lumen). Talos does not equate UAT-11473 with Volt Typhoon. Lumen lists CVE-2026-20127 among Silent Train's exploited vulnerabilities; activity runs roughly 09:00–22:00 China Standard Time.

### Timeline
- 25 Feb 2026: Cisco advisory published. No intrusion dates given ("historical activity").
- 28 Oct 17:32: Talos report. 28 Oct 21:54 GMT: Cisco advisory v2.1 links it.
- Talos does not say whether the CVE was used as a zero-day here.

### Initial access
- Exploitation of CVE-2026-20127 on Catalyst SD-WAN Controller.

### Tooling
- **PolyFlame** (`libdssl.so`): injected into vdaemon; hooks recvfrom, sendto, getpeername, dlopen, dlsym, mprotect; TinyShell-style backdoor and intermediate-C2 relay.
- **rsshd:** ptrace injector. **conf_sshd:** shell script that finds vdaemon PIDs and drives injection.
- **Packed aaamgrd** (vBond): static ELF64, RC4-encrypted embedded PolyFlame, direct syscalls, exits if a debugger is attached (TracerPid), PAM tampering.
- **LittleFilch** (`libcommon.so`, vSmart): OpenSSL SSL_read hook extracting public IP, peer OMP system-IP and 32-byte IPsec keys; writes RSA-encrypted records to `/var/tmp/vdaemon.bin`.
- Timestomping with `touch --reference=` and `chmod --reference=`.

### Persistence
- Trojanised runit scripts under `/etc/sv/sshd/` and `/etc/sv/aaamgr/` remount `/rootfs.rw` executable and launch conf_sshd before the real sshd; the aaamgr path deletes overlay PAM configs for sshd and login.

### C2
- No hardcoded IPs or domains. Tasking runs in-band over SD-WAN control-plane traffic; PolyFlame checks an MD5 of the date (YYYY-MM-DD) against the device's local date. Backdoor commands 0x100 exec, 0x200 write, 0x300 read; relay commands 0x403–0x408. LittleFilch exfiltrates over UDP in DTLS 1.2-style records to endpoints in `vdaemon.conf`, which is deleted after reading.

### IOCs (as published; Talos published no hashes, IPs or domains)
- Paths/files: `/tmp/system-vdaemon`, `/rootfs.rw`, `/var/tmp/vdaemon.bin`, `/var/tmp/vdaemon.conf`, `/etc/sv/sshd/`, `/etc/sv/aaamgr/`, `libdssl.so`, `libcommon.so`, `conf_sshd`, `rsshd`, `aaamgrd`
- LittleFilch byte markers: `40 03 06`, `40 0C 04`, `05 01 0C 06 40`; first decrypted byte 0x07; length threshold 0x18F
- Snort: CVE-2026-20127 SIDs 65938, 65958; KeyDrift Snort 2 SIDs 67387–67416; Snort 3 7:44–7:73

### Hunting
1. Diff `/etc/sv/sshd/run` and `/etc/sv/aaamgr/run` against known-good images; look for `mount -o remount` of `/rootfs.rw` or background launches before sshd.
2. Non-standard shared objects (`libdssl.so`, `libcommon.so`) mapped in vdaemon (`/proc/<pid>/maps`, or Cisco TAC tooling).
3. ptrace attachment to vdaemon; presence of `/var/tmp/vdaemon.bin` or `vdaemon.conf`.
4. Missing or modified overlay PAM files for sshd and login; files whose mtime exactly matches a reference file.
5. Controller logs for CVE-2026-20127 indicators: unexpected peering or control connections, new admin accounts.
6. Control-plane peers or UDP flows to unexpected endpoints; management access from SOHO/VPS sources.

### Response
1. Patch to 20.9.8.2, 20.12.5.3/20.12.6.1, 20.15.4.2 or 20.18.2.1; apply Cisco's SD-WAN hardening guide.
2. Capture admin-tech or memory before reboot or upgrade.
3. If implants are found, engage Cisco TAC and rebuild controllers from known-good images.
4. Assume overlay IPsec keys and control-session material are compromised: rotate keys and certificates across the overlay and re-onboard edges.
5. Restrict controller management to trusted networks; audit for rogue edges or peers.
6. If the SD-WAN is run by a managed service provider, ask them these questions directly.
