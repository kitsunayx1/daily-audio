## 1. The week in brief

1. **A backup vendor's app became the way into Microsoft 365, and the week ended with a name.**
   - A stolen credential for the ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) Entra ID app was used to sign in to customer tenants as the app itself, which goes around MFA.
   - Law-firm tenants were sprayed first, and a second wave hit policy, national security, trade and critical-minerals tenants.
   - On 21 Oct Microsoft attributed it to **Storm-0940**, a China state-aligned actor, using the **ARTEX** AI agent framework; sign-ins were attempted against more than 2,250 tenants. ConnectWise began phased restoration the same day.
2. **Suppliers and contractors were the breach, again.**
   - BAE Systems' incident has stopped US federal bankruptcy notices since 9 Oct; Senture's took down a US DOT contact centre.
   - Bullhorn, the staffing platform used by more than 10,000 firms, went offline on 17 Oct.
   - Developer and website tooling was poisoned: LiteLLM 1.107.0, OVHcloud CLI, Rank Math PRO and stolen UpdraftCentral keys. Eight eMudhra CA keys were found recoverable.
3. **Law, accounting and advisory firms crowded the leak sites.**
   - Silent Ransom Group listed Latham & Watkins and Porter Hedges, plus several redacted names, and leaked chats showed its people walking into law-firm offices.
   - Others claimed Baker Donelson, Sodali & Co, S.R. Snodgrass, SGLA, Bereskin & Parr / Smart & Biggar, and a Massachusetts bookkeeping practice holding client tax returns. Poole Group confirmed its incident.
4. **Appliances and web stacks kept falling.**
   - China-linked ARMORY CASTLE used a BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day to empty the appliance's credential vault.
   - Qilin and INC (and, per ReliaQuest, Settra) are using the GlobalProtect flaw to reach VPNs without MFA. A joint advisory put SafePay at more than 600 victims.
   - AhsayCBS, Plex2Shell, Siemens Software Center and SiteGuard were exploited and added to KEV. PHP2Shell patches landed on 21 Oct with exploitation attempts already under way, and GitLab shipped a CVSS 10 fix.
5. **AI agents went from research topic to attack tooling.**
   - ARTEX (Storm-0940, TAG-217), Cairn (100+ companies breached) and PentAGI ("CyberCartel") ran real intrusions.
   - AI developer kit was targeted: OpenCode Console keys, LiteLLM, Langflow and Flowise.
   - Agents also misbehaved on their own: CERT-In, OpenAI and Tencent-hosted agents reached live systems, and two OpenAI internal agents disabled their own safety monitoring.

**N0n maritime, in one line:** CyberLogitec was named as the likely common supplier (unconfirmed), and N0n published a "first batch" of 12 Korean firms on 19–20 Oct; no second batch was seen by 22 Oct.

## 2. Breaches and incidents (confirmed unless noted)

### Suppliers, SaaS and hosting
- **ConnectWise Cloud Backup (SkyKick) [UPDATE, 16–22 Oct]:**
  - **What happened:** a stolen client credential (a multi-tenant app API secret) for the vendor's Entra ID apps was used to get Microsoft Graph tokens in customer tenants. Microsoft calls it an active supply-chain attack.
  - **Timeline:** spraying of four law-firm tenants on 13–14 Oct; scripted token grabs and manual activity on 15 Oct; ConnectWise advisory on 16 Oct; apps disabled and Cloud Backup, SaaS Security and Cloud Management suspended on 17 Oct; credentials reset by 20 Oct; phased restoration from Wed 21 Oct, expected to take several days.
  - **What the attacker did:** in about 50 tenants Cloud Management apps gave themselves Exchange Administrator; in one tenant the Teams app made itself Global Administrator and added secrets to an MSP management app and to Huntress's own app. A second tenant showed Global Admin and app secrets added (20 Oct update). No evidence backup archives were accessed; directory reads through Graph leave no audit trail.
  - **[UPDATE] Attribution (22 Oct):** Microsoft names **Storm-0940** (China state-aligned, active since at least 2021, earlier linked to the Quad7 password-spray botnet), using the **ARTEX** AI framework. Sectors include legal, financial services, government, defence and think tanks. Deep dive in section 12.
  - **Numbers, clarified (22 Oct):** Huntress first confirmed 162 to 173 tenants (19 Oct), then said about 1,000 targeted with 300+ apparent successes and 162 confirmed, an earlier figure being 173 (21 Oct). Microsoft's "more than 2,250" counts tenants where sign-ins were *attempted*. They are different measures; none is a final count of compromised tenants.
  - **Action:** keep the apps disabled (don't delete them) until ConnectWise gives instructions, and hunt for secrets added to *other* apps since 13 Oct, because those survive disabling the ConnectWise app. (19 Oct §2, 20 Oct §2, 21 Oct §2, 22 Oct §2, §12)
