## 1. The week in five lines

1. **Edge devices were the main way in.** Ivanti Connect Secure (a zero-day chain that rooted fully patched VPNs, now mass-scanned), Palo Alto PAN-OS and GlobalProtect, Cisco Secure Email Gateway, SonicWall SMA 1000 and Atlassian Data Center were all exploited. Unit 42's PEERSTITCH report showed how far a FortiManager compromise can reach.
2. **Professional services were a steady target.**
   - Deloitte is on The Gentlemen's leak site, with no data posted yet.
   - Silent Ransom Group (Luna Moth) sent people into law firms posing as IT, and claims Baker McKenzie.
   - An Accenture contractor's missed patch let ShinyHunters breach FBIjobs.gov.
   - The EY breach fallout continued.
   - Auditors, accountants, architects and advisers appeared on leak sites every day.
3. **European retail and food were hit hard.** Danfoss and Copeland refrigeration controllers were sabotaged across hundreds of French and European supermarkets. Ahold Delhaize, TFG London, Air France-KLM and H&M (which denies a compromise) also had incidents or claims. Fehmarnbelt tunnel construction IT was halted.
4. **Threats are moving off the screen.**
   - A Semperis survey says 37% of ransomware attacks involved threats against staff and families.
   - The Revolut KYC leak may be linked to a home invasion (unconfirmed).
   - An OpenAI engineer was charged over a hidden conference-room recorder.
   - The DOJ shut down more than 40 North Korean IT-worker laptop farms.
5. **AI kept lowering the cost of offence.**
   - CyberXero ran 51 Claude Code agents.
   - XBOW built a Chrome full chain in six days with a public model.
   - Booz Allen's AI agents took over OT labs in minutes.
   - AI found flaws in curl, the Linux kernel and Windows, and LiteLLM gateways were found vulnerable.
